# Largest-ready-first scheduling on the primary Ruff machine

Cargo started the largest eligible native link first among already ready jobs of equal priority. On the 32-CPU C5 x86-64 host this helped. On the independently registered 32-CPU H8 host it hurt. Both ran eight Cargo jobs, the same two profile-guided Cargo binaries, the same rustc and the same stock Wild. The candidate never skips a compiler or linker. The registered condition required both machines to benefit. The primary endpoint was the geometric mean of adjacent paired ratios. The two independently warmed 48-pair batches improved 7.67% and 7.49%. The candidate won 69 of 96 pairs, with two-sided exact sign p = 0.0000215. On H8 the independent 96-pair result was an 11.87% slowdown (95% interval 8.15–15.86% slower), and its plain-output series was 18.14% slower. The opposite effects occurred in both output modes. C5 stationary batches used writable ext4 storage. Its independent edit and restore arms used writable tmpfs. Their raw times have different denominators. The measurements do not establish a general policy keyed to host identity or storage type.
